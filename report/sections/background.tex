\section{Background}
A layout assigns the $n$ vertices of a simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$
to distinct positions $0,\ldots,n-1$ on a circle. For positions $i,j$ the
distance is $d_n(i,j)=\min(|i-j|,n-|i-j|)$. The bandwidth of a layout
is the largest distance across an edge, with value zero for an empty edge
set. The optimization problem minimizes this bandwidth over all layouts.

For an integer threshold $K$, the decision problem asks whether a layout
of bandwidth at most $K$ exists. Each position has at most $2K$ distinct
neighbours within distance $K$, so $\lceil\Delta(G)/2\rceil$ is a lower
bound on the optimum. Every permutation has bandwidth at most
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. A validated layout of value $B$ gives an upper bound.
Equality with a proven lower bound, or a proof of infeasibility at $B-1$,
certifies optimality. An interrupted decision leaves that threshold unresolved.

\subsection{Related work}
Ren et al.~\cite{Ren2020CBMP} use iterated local search to find labelings
with small cyclic bandwidth. Their algorithm improves a labeling locally
and uses two perturbation strategies to explore other labelings when the
search stalls. On 85 benchmark instances with known optima, it matched
the optimal bandwidth in 82 cases.

A labeling found by local search gives an upper bound $B$. Our pairwise
and order SAT encodings address the complementary question of whether
any labeling can improve that bound. At threshold $K=B-1$, a satisfying
assignment yields a better layout, while an unsatisfiability proof
certifies that $B$ is optimal. JGL009 and BCSSTK01 also appear in the
benchmark set of Ren et al. We use them to study the cost of constructing
the two encodings and the bounds each solver reaches within its time budget.
