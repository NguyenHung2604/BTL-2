\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Comparing the SAT encodings at a common threshold}
For $n\ge 2$ and $K\ge 0$, both encodings forbid the same ordered position
pairs on each edge. Before simplification, their edge clauses therefore
have the same count. The assignment clauses account for the difference:
pairwise uses $n+2n\binom{n}{2}$, while order uses
$n(n-2)+n\binom{n}{2}$. A pairwise edge clause contains two literals.
Excluding an interior position in the order encoding takes two threshold
literals per endpoint, so an order edge clause can contain four.

On BCSSTK01 ($n=48$, $m=176$), each position at $K=23$ excludes only
its diametrical opposite. Both encodings therefore add $176\cdot48=8{,}448$
edge clauses. Order used 44.5\% fewer clauses overall, but took longer
to construct and load in this observation (Table~\ref{tab:k23}).
Figure~\ref{fig:thresholds} compares only thresholds visited by both runs ($K=23$ down to 12).
Each unit decrease in $K$ forbids two more partners per position and adds
$2\cdot48\cdot176=16{,}896$ edge clauses to either formula. The assignment
clauses are independent of $K$, so the two lines are parallel, separated
by 51,984 clauses throughout. The relative reduction of 44.5\% applies
only at $K=23$.

\subsection{From formula size to search outcomes}
\begin{samepage}
Across all thresholds on BCSSTK01, pairwise built 13 formulas with
2,836,080 clauses in 3.390 seconds and reached bandwidth 12. Order built
12 formulas with 1,892,736 clauses in 5.746 seconds and reached bandwidth
13. These totals sum the input clauses over successive builds. The final
decisions at $K=11$ and $K=12$, respectively, timed out, leaving both
SAT runs with the degree lower bound of 6. In the six-method 300-second run, CP-SAT returned 12 with a stored lower
bound of 0; the degree bound raises the reported lower bound to 6.
In the separate day-8 pilot, the CP-SAT adapter recorded OPTIMAL at 12.
Thus the pairwise layout attained the optimum, although its own run
ended with bounds $[6,12]$.
\end{samepage}

\subsection{Limits of the comparison}
Only BCSSTK01 exercised both SAT encodings in the three-graph rerun.
JGL009 closes at the degree bound and BP~400 triggers the guard.
The supplement adds two informative cases: both encodings proved 4 on
BCSPWR01 and returned $[6,9]$ on IBM32. IBM CP Optimizer also reached 9 on IBM32, but tightened the lower
bound to 7. On BCSPWR01 both CP engines and both SAT encodings proved 4;
Gurobi stopped with bounds $[3,6]$. These few observations cannot
isolate the effect of clause width or support a general runtime ranking.
