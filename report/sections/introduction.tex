\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Consider six positions around a table. Positions 0 and 5 are adjacent, just
as positions 0 and 1 are. If an edge of a graph joins two vertices, their
separation in such a layout is the length of the shorter arc between their
positions. The cyclic bandwidth minimization problem (CBMP) asks how small
the largest of these edge separations can be when each vertex receives a
distinct position.

A layout gives an upper bound that can be checked by inspecting its edges.
A claim of optimality also requires evidence that every layout with a smaller
bandwidth is impossible. This distinction matters when a solver reaches its
time limit. A run can yield a useful layout even while the optimum remains
unknown.

The two SAT encodings express the same threshold decision using different
variables. Pairwise encoding asks whether a vertex occupies a particular
position. Order encoding asks whether its position is at most a given
threshold. We compare their formula sizes and construction times at a
common bandwidth threshold, then examine the layouts and bounds obtained
under a budget for the full optimization run.

Section~\ref{sec:pairwise} derives the direct encoding and its clause
count. Small graphs with exhaustive reference answers test correctness.
Three graphs derived from Harwell--Boeing matrices illustrate how the
degree bound, formula size and available solver resources affect which
instances reach search. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} relates these cases
to the measured cost of each encoding.
