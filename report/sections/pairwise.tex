\section{Pairwise encoding}
\label{sec:pairwise}

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple undirected graph on $n$ vertices and let
$P=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ be the available positions. For a bijection $p:V\to P$,
the distance between two positions is
$d_n(i,j)=\min(|i-j|,n-|i-j|)$. The bandwidth $B(p)$ is the maximum of
$d_n(p(u),p(v))$ over all edges $\{u,v\}$, with $B(p)=0$ when $E$ is empty.
For an integer threshold $K$, the decision formula $F(K)$ asks whether
there exists a labeling with $B(p)\le K$.

\subsection{Clauses and decoding}
For each $v\in V$ and $i\in P$, the Boolean variable $x_{v,i}$ means
$p(v)=i$. The implementation numbers it $1+vn+i$, so a positive SAT
literal identifies both the vertex and its position. For $K\ge 0$ the
formula consists of four clause families:
\begin{align}
&\bigvee_{i\in P}x_{v,i}
    && (v\in V), \label{eq:pw-alo}\\
&\neg x_{v,i}\lor\neg x_{v,j}
    && (v\in V,\ i<j), \label{eq:pw-row}\\
&\neg x_{u,i}\lor\neg x_{v,i}
    && (u<v,\ i\in P), \label{eq:pw-col}\\
&\neg x_{u,i}\lor\neg x_{v,j}
    && (\{u,v\}\in E,\ d_n(i,j)>K). \label{eq:pw-edge}
\end{align}
In~\eqref{eq:pw-edge} each edge is taken once in a fixed orientation and
$(i,j)$ ranges over ordered pairs of positions. On the six-cycle at $K=1$,
an edge permits positions $(0,5)$ and forbids $(0,3)$. The natural
labeling is feasible since all six edges, including the closing edge, have
distance one. At $K=0$ even a single edge between distinct vertices makes
the formula unsatisfiable. For $K<0$ the encoder adds an empty clause,
including on edgeless inputs.

\paragraph{Correctness.}
Given a feasible labeling, set exactly the variable $x_{v,p(v)}$ in each
row to true. Distinct labels satisfy~\eqref{eq:pw-col}, and the bandwidth
bound ensures that each clause in~\eqref{eq:pw-edge} has at least one true negative literal.
Conversely, \eqref{eq:pw-alo} and~\eqref{eq:pw-row} select one position
per vertex. By~\eqref{eq:pw-col} the $n$ selected positions are distinct;
hence all $n$ positions are occupied. Reading the unique true variable in
row $v$ gives $p(v)$. A decoded edge of distance greater than $K$ would
violate~\eqref{eq:pw-edge}. Thus the decoded permutation has bandwidth at
most $K$. The implementation independently validates the permutation and
recomputes its bandwidth before accepting it.

\paragraph{Size of one decision instance.}
There are $n^2$ variables, $n$ row disjunctions, and
$2n\binom{n}{2}$ pairwise assignment clauses. For $n\ge 1$ and $K\ge 0$,
each position has $\min(n,2K+1)$ allowed partner positions, counting itself.
Writing $m=|E|$, the total number of clauses before solver simplification is
\[
  n+2n\binom{n}{2}+mn\bigl(n-\min(n,2K+1)\bigr).
\]
The last term vanishes at $K=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For the six-cycle at
$K=1$ the count is $6+180+108=294$ clauses on 36 variables. The pilot's
cumulative clause count sums the completed builds across all thresholds.

\subsection{Searching for the optimum}
Start with the identity permutation, validate it and let $B$ be its measured
bandwidth. If $\Delta$ is the maximum degree (zero for an empty graph), then
$L=\lceil\Delta/2\rceil$ is a lower bound: at most $2K$ other positions
can lie within distance $K$ of a vertex. Every permutation also satisfies
$B\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

While $B>L$, build $F(B-1)$ and start a fresh Glucose3 instance. A
satisfying model gives a validated layout with measured bandwidth $B'\le B-1$;
set $B=B'$ and use this value for the next threshold. If $F(B-1)$ is
unsatisfiable, no better integer bandwidth exists and $B$ is optimal.
Equality $B=L$ also proves optimality and may occur before any SAT call.
One wall-clock budget covers construction and search at every threshold.
An interrupted decision is recorded as \texttt{UNKNOWN}; the search retains
the best validated layout and the last justified lower bound. For example,
in the pilot on \texttt{bcsstk01}, a layout reached $B=12$, and the call at
$K=11$ timed out. The recorded interval was $[6,12]$.
